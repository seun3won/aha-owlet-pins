Before you start

  • Most babies start solids at about 6 months; before 4 months is not recommended.
  • Offer one new single-ingredient food at a time and wait 3–5 days between new foods, so you can spot any problems.
  • Common allergens can be introduced alongside other foods. If your baby has severe eczema or an egg allergy, talk to your doctor before peanut.
  • No honey before 12 months. Cow's milk as a drink waits until after 12 months.
  • Serve soft textures, cut round foods (grapes, cherries, tomatoes) small, and always watch your baby while they eat.

Where the numbers come from
  • CDC — When, What, and How to Introduce Solid Foods (about 6 months, 3–5 days between new foods, allergens)
  • CDC — Foods and Drinks to Avoid or Limit (no honey before 12 months)
  • CDC — Choking Hazards

The food list is a checklist for variety, not a feeding plan, and “100” is a fun target, not a requirement. Prepare every food in a texture that is safe for your baby's stage. This is general information, not medical or nutrition advice — talk to your pediatrician about starting solids, allergies, or any reaction.
