Short, source-checked answers to the questions parents actually type into a search box. No signup, no newsletter wall. Every child is different, and none of this replaces advice from your pediatrician.

  • Why Does a Baby Have More Bones Than You?

    Babies are born with around three hundred bones and adults end up with two hundred and six. Many of them fuse together as a child grows.

  • Log Feeds and Naps in One Tap

    A Notion baby planner built for tired parents, with a one page meltdown SOS you scan in the moment instead of writing up afterward.

  • Why Big Baby Eyes Make You Want to Pick Them Up

    Large eyes, round cheeks and a small chin. The pull you feel toward that set of features is measurable, and it is not just you.

  • Why Do Newborns Stare at Black and White?

    Colour vision develops gradually over a baby&#x27;s first months, which is why high contrast black and white patterns are the easiest thing for a newborn to focus on.

  • Does a Newborn Really Copy Your Face?

    Stick your tongue out at a days old baby and watch what happens. The finding is decades old and still argued about, which is what makes it worth trying yourself.

  • When Do Babies Start Crying Real Tears?

    Newborns cry hard and dry. Tear glands take weeks to reach full volume, so most babies do not shed visible tears until somewhere between two weeks and three months.

  • How Far Can a Newborn Actually See?

    Newborn vision reaches about eight to twelve inches, roughly the distance from your arms to your face. Here is why that distance is not an accident.

  • Why Paying Your Kid Can Backfire

    Reward something a child already enjoys and the enjoyment can drop once the reward stops. The classic name for it is the overjustification effect.

    A free printable guide to the simple back and forth pattern between you and your baby that researchers keep pointing to. No app and no schedule needed.

  • Why You Use a Sing-Song Voice With a Baby

    Infants prefer the sing song voice adults slip into, and they were showing that preference in studies long before anyone told parents to do it.

  • Why Unfinished Things Stay Stuck in Your Head

    Interrupted tasks are remembered better than finished ones. Useful to know at nine at night when the day is only half done.

  • Why Does Your Baby Look in the Wrong Cup?

    Your baby watched you move it and still reaches where they found it last time. It is a known stage of infant development, not a mistake.
